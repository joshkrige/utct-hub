⚠ Compulsory gear — UT100 (official list)
18 compulsory items, carried the whole race. Random checks on course, and penalties are 15–30 minutes per missing item. The organiser states plainly: this list is never changed or downgraded based on the weather forecast. You carry full mountain kit in 25°C regardless.

Four things most likely to catch you out:
▶ The jacket. It must be seam-sealed with an integrated hood. A softshell or windproof fails the check, however good it is. Verify yours before race week, not at registration.
▶ 350 lumens. Check your headtorch’s actual rating, and carry the spare batteries — they are part of the requirement, not a nicety.
▶ 2 litres of capacity. Not 1.5 — the 1.5l line is for the shorter races. Make sure your pack genuinely carries 2l.
▶ The two reserves. 300ml of water and 250 calories must be on you at all times, including crossing the finish line. Easy to eat or drink your way into a penalty in the last 10km.
🏃 Train with it. Full waterproofs, thermals, gloves, beanie, space blanket and 2l of water is a real pack weight — and you carry it for 20 hours whatever the forecast says. Every long run from here should be done with the full race pack so nothing is a surprise. The 13 Peaks day especially.
✅ Not required for UT100: a second headlamp or backup torch — that is the 100-miler only. One lamp plus spare batteries is enough.
Recommended but not compulsory: race belt, peak cap, sunglasses, sunscreen, ziplock bags, wet wipes, anti-chafe cream, disinfectant, R400 cash or a card.

Other rules: crew may support you only within 100m of an aid station (30-minute penalty otherwise). No pacing or muling. No swapping packs. Take as much food and drink from aid stations as you like, but not their cups, bottles or cans. If you DNF, reach the nearest aid station and tell the station captain yourself. Accepting an IV from the medical crew is an automatic DQ. Cutoffs require you to depart before the barrier, not merely arrive.
🌞 Weather — the south-easter
Late November is peak season for the Cape Doctor, the south-easterly that funnels over Table Mountain and down the Atlantic seaboard. The organiser’s own warning is that the weather can change drastically once you are on the mountain.
Where it will hit you. The exposed sections are Signal Hill and Lions Head in the first hour, the Table Mountain plateau around km 25–31, and the Twelve Apostles traverse toward Llandudno. A strong south-easter on the plateau is genuinely cold even on a hot day, and it makes the contour paths slow going.

What it changes. Wind-chill at 04:00 on an exposed ridge is a different proposition from the 25°C you will meet at Hout Bay six hours later. Plan a windproof layer you can put on and take off without stopping. Wind also dries you out faster than you notice — your 300ml reserve rule matters more, not less, on a windy day.

Check it on the Thursday and again race morning, and set your layers from that rather than from the forecast you looked at a week out.

Strength circuit — under 60 min

Warm-up: 5 min dynamic (skips, leg swings, hip circles). Minimal rest between exercises, short rest between rounds.

Thursday core & stability — 40 min

Complements Tuesday’s leg circuit. No poles means no upper-body work — this is core, single-leg stability and ankle resilience instead. Those are what keep you upright on technical ground at hour 18, and what stop a rolled ankle ending the race. 4 rounds, 60 s rest between rounds. No heavy leg work: legs stay fresh for Friday.

⚡ Race day fuel — sub-20h
The scale of it: 20 hours at 70g of carbohydrate per hour is roughly 1,400g of carbs. That is why this gets rehearsed rather than planned. Use glucose-fructose products — glucose alone saturates its transporter near 60g/h, and the fructose pathway is how you get above that ceiling.
Carbohydrate
60–80g per hour, from hour one. Do not wait until you feel like eating. Set a watch alarm if needed.
Sodium
500–800mg/h baseline. Push toward 1,000mg/h through the midday heat (roughly hours 7–11).
Fluid
500–750ml/h, more in the heat of the day. Clear to pale-yellow urine is the check.
🍕 02:30 breakfast
80–100g carbs, low fibre, low fat. Eating properly at that hour is a trained skill — rehearse it at least twice before race week. Another 30g at about 03:45.
Solids
Front-load them. Bars, dates, PB sandwiches while the stomach is willing — roughly the first 8 hours.
Gels & liquid carb
Take over through the hot middle and the dark finish, when chewing stops being appealing.
Coke
From about hour 12. One cup per station through to the finish.
☕ Caffeine
Save it for the dark. Nothing meaningful before hour 12. Then 50–100mg every few hours through the final four hours on the headtorch.
Nothing new
After the last long run on Fri 13 Nov, no new products. Race on what you rehearsed.
Race phase strategy — by aid station
START → Kloof Nek · km 20 · 0–3h · 04:00–07:00: Headtorch to about 05:30. Signal Hill then Kloof Nek, 757m of climbing. It is cool, you are fresh, and it will feel far too easy — good. Eat from hour one anyway. Kloof Nek cutoff is 08:00 and you should be an hour clear.
→ Table Mountain · km 31 · 3–6h: Platteklip Gorge — 876m in under 5km. The steepest section of the whole race. Power-hike from the bottom, eat at the top, do not race anyone up it. Twenty minutes per kilometre here is on schedule, not slow.
→ Hout Bay · km 57 · 6–11h · 10:00–15:00: Llandudno and Rocket Road. Runnable, and this is where the heat arrives — you are out here through the hottest hours. Sodium toward 1,000mg/h. Hout Bay is your only drop bag: restock everything, change socks, pick up the torch. Keep the stop under ten minutes.
→ Constantia Glen · km 72 · 11–14h · THE CRUX: 945m of climbing over 15km — the biggest single stage on the course, arriving when you are eleven hours in. Everything you saved in the first half is for this. Walk the climbs deliberately, keep eating, and expect it to take over three hours.
→ FINISH · km 99 · 14–20h: Alphen is a brief flat reprieve, then Nursery Ravine. Torch back on around km 80. Newlands, then Dead Man’s Tree stings you for 487m between km 88 and 96 — do not be surprised by it. Caffeine from here. Then 2.87km downhill into the city and done.
🔥 Heat adaptation calendar
Why, and how much. A 04:00 start puts you on the mountain through the hottest part of race day, around seven to nine hours in. No sauna needed — overdressed midday runs do the job, though they are a weaker stimulus than a sauna protocol. Nine sessions across 17 days, which sits inside the 7–10 that produce most of the adaptation. Loaded into the recovery week rather than the sharpening week, because heat is low-impact but it is not free.
Method
Run 11:00–15:00, one extra layer, 30–60 min, easy effort only. Never on a quality session. Never the day before a long run.
