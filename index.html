💤 Sleep & caffeine strategy
No planned sleep stop. Sub-40h is achievable without sleep if you manage caffeine and energy correctly. Your job is to never let yourself stop moving for more than 10 minutes at any aid station.
Caffeine timing
Hours 0–10 (Fri 17:00–03:00): no caffeine. Running on natural alertness — your first night is manageable.
Hours 11–14 (Hout Bay to Silvermine): start Coke and caffeine gels. Begin early, before you feel you need it.
Hours 14–30 (Sat afternoon through Sat night): Coke at every crew stop — this is the deepest sleep window of the race. Kalk Bay, Simon’s Town, Scarborough, Kommetjie, Noordhoek — all Coke.
Hours 31–40 (Sun 00:00–finish): reduce caffeine. You finish ~09:00 Sun — too much now wrecks the recovery sleep you need immediately after.
Managing sleepiness on course
▶ Brief stop (2 min) + cold water on face + solid food usually resets the brain enough to continue.
▶ If you get micro-sleeps (head nodding while moving): 10 min lying flat at the next crew point + caffeine is faster net time than shuffling at 2 km/h for an hour.
▶ Brief crew: watch for slurred speech, stumbling on flat ground, glazed stare. These are “stop and reset” signals — override Josh if needed.

💤 Sleep & caffeine strategy
⚠ Two full nights. The 45h plan runs through Fri night AND Sat night. By Simon’s Town (~23:30 Sat, hour 30) you will have been awake ~35+ hours. A tactical nap here is not a concession — it is a strategy.
Why 20–30 min — and not more
Sleep cycles are ~90 minutes. The danger zone is 45–60 min: you enter deep slow-wave sleep but don’t complete the cycle, and wake feeling significantly worse than before — disoriented, heavy-legged, and hard to restart. Avoid this window completely.
20–30 min takes you into Stage 2 (light restorative sleep) and out again cleanly. Real restoration, minimal grogginess. This is the correct choice regardless of your fitness level — it’s sleep cycle science, not elite privilege.
A full 90-min cycle would feel best, but costs too much time on the cutoff plan.
Nap 1 — Simon’s Town (hour 30, ~23:30 Sat)
If coherent and moving steadily: press on. Eat and go.
If shuffling, stumbling, or micro-sleeping: 20–25 min nap now. Lie flat, crew sets alarm immediately. Eat + Coke before you stand. Net cost ~35 min; shuffling at 2 km/h for 63km costs you hours.

Nap 2 — Scarborough or Kommetjie (hours 33–35, 02:30–04:30 Sun)
If you slept at Simon’s Town and still fading: a second 20-min nap here is valid. You are far enough from the finish cutoff that the buffer holds.
Do not nap at Noordhoek (hour 37, 06:30 Sun) or later — cutoffs are too tight.
The week before: bank sleep aggressively
Sleep debt going into the race compounds badly at hour 25+. Aim for 8–9 hours Mon–Thu. Race night (Thu) and pre-race night (Fri) you will sleep badly — that is normal and manageable if you arrive well-rested. Do not underestimate this.
Caffeine protocol
Hours 0–15: none to minimal — preserve sensitivity for later.
Hours 16–36 (Hout Bay through Sat night): Coke at every crew stop without exception.
Hours 36–45 (Sun morning to finish): ease off — you need real sleep immediately after finishing.
Caffeine masks fatigue but does not restore motor function. A severely sleep-deprived brain on caffeine will feel more confident while making worse decisions. Nap first, caffeine after.
Crew instructions
▶ Alarm set before Josh closes his eyes. 20 min. No exceptions.
▶ On waking: food and Coke in hand before he stands. Do not let him lie there "just one more minute."
▶ Do not let him lie down on the trail unsupervised. One unalarmed rest can become 90 min.
▶ Intervene at: slurred speech, stumbling on flat ground, blank stare, answering questions with a delay.
👀 Night vision protocol (glasses problem)
The glasses problem: Forehead headtorch shines through lenses — creates glare and reflection, nearly useless in mist. You run 15+ hours in darkness.

Best fix: Daily disposable contact lenses for night sections. Test from Wk 8. Bring glasses as backup.
If contacts not possible: Chest-mounted secondary light eliminates the reflection. Anti-fog cloth in hip pockets and Hout Bay drop bag. Practice in fog and rain now.
Winter bonus: Every dark morning run = free night training. Start your headtorch setup today.
